7Billing process

Menu Sales. Factory commercial chain — the invoice always comes before the delivery note:

Proforma
➜
Sales Order
➜
Invoice
➜
Delivery Note
Mandatory ruleNo invoice, no delivery note. The DN is issued only after the invoice (from the invoiced order). Never deliver without a prior invoice.
  1. Proforma invoice: quotation / offer → Validate → Confirm → Approve.
  2. Sales order: customer order confirmation.
  3. Invoice: starts the real sale; print for the customer; settle cash or credit.
  4. Delivery note: created after the invoice; authorises shipment of FG / merchandise; impacts stock on approval.

7.1 Cash or credit settlement

ModeProcedure
CashImmediate collection on the invoice. Select the account: cash or bank.
CreditInvoice on account. Customer needs a ledger account. Then DN to deliver. Follow-up under Credits / Recovery.

9Accounting — chart & treasury

9.1 Chart of Accounts

  1. Open Chart of Accounts and review the hierarchy.
  2. Each account has a code, name and type: Asset, Liability, Equity, Income, Expense.
  3. Account parameters map operations (sales, stock, VAT, cash, bank) to the correct codes.
  4. Do not rename or delete an account that already has movements without Finance approval.

9.2 Daily treasury

9Accounting — depreciation

Fixed assets (mills, vehicles, buildings, production equipment) lose value over time. Depreciation recognises that expense each period.

ConceptDetail
Fixed asset / PPELong-term asset used in operations (e.g. mill, truck, building).
CostAcquisition cost (purchase + capitalised incidental costs if configured).
Useful lifeYears (or months) over which cost is allocated.
Depreciation chargePeriod expense: Debit Depreciation expense / Credit Accumulated depreciation.
Net book value (NBV)Cost − Accumulated depreciation.

9.4 Practical steps

  1. Register the fixed asset in the asset / account structure as configured.
  2. Set useful life and method (straight-line is usual for training).
  3. At period or year end, post the depreciation charge.
  4. Check the charge on the income statement (P&L) and NBV on the balance sheet.
NoteDo not depreciate inventory (RM, FG, merchandise). Only fixed assets are depreciated. Inventory is valued at cost / production cost.

9Accounting — year-end closing

Year-end closing closes one financial period and opens the next. Restricted to Finance / authorised administrators.

9.5 Closing checklist

  1. Physical inventory of RM, materials, FG, merchandise — reconcile to the system.
  2. Bank reconciliation and cash count (balances = reality).
  3. Post adjusting entries: accruals, prepayments, provisions if required.
  4. Post depreciation charges for the period.
  5. Review AR / AP balances (customers and suppliers).
  6. Print / export: Trial balance, Income statement (P&L), Balance sheet (Active + Passive).
  7. Close the year: income and expense accounts are closed; net profit or loss goes to retained earnings.
  8. Open the new year: balance-sheet balances are brought forward as opening balances.
ReportUse
Accounts Balance (Trial Balance)All accounts with Debit / Credit balances — first control before close.
Income statement (P&L)Income − Expenses = profit or loss for the period.
Active Balance SheetAssets: inventory, receivables, cash/bank, fixed assets at NBV.
Passive Balance SheetEquity, payables, tax/social liabilities, other liabilities.
SecurityNever close without written Finance approval. Take a backup before final closing. After close, do not alter closed-period movements without an authorised reopening procedure.

11Best practices and exercises
